Why older dogs

It started with Lucy

Lucy was twelve years old when she came home with me for good. Her first mom had just passed away, and her whole world had turned upside down. Plenty of people would have looked at a scruffy twelve-year-old terrier and decided her best days were behind her.

Lucy had other plans. She gave me five and a half more years of stubborn sniffing expeditions, happy jumps and squirrel chases. She leaned on me through the hard times, and I leaned on her. Some of the best years of my life were the last years of hers.

Lucy was lucky. She had someone who had promised to love her. So many older dogs don't. They lose their person, or their family can't keep them anymore, and they end up waiting in a kennel, confused and lonely, watching the younger dogs go home first.

The dream

I want to build a place for those dogs. Maybe it starts as a pet hotel, maybe as a rescue, but either way it will feel like a real home, not a kennel: a couch to curl up on, a sunny spot to nap in, walks that go at their pace, and someone to scratch that perfect spot behind their ears every single day.

Some of them will find families of their own, and watching them go home will be the best kind of goodbye. Some of them will stay with me, and they'll spend the rest of their days safe, warm, unhurried and loved. No dog should have to spend their last chapter alone.

I'm not there yet. But every walk, every drop-in and every overnight I do for your pets is a step toward it. When you trust me with your dog, you're helping build a home for a dog who doesn't have one yet. That means more to me than I can say.
